# Screening methodology

This document is the plain-English source of truth for what the screener does.
Every number below comes from `config/`. When a threshold changes there, update it
here too.

## 1. Pipeline

1. **Universe.** Keep investable U.S. operating companies only (§2).
2. **Metrics.** Compute 70+ metrics per company from standardized statements and market data. Each metric gets a status code (§3).
3. **Profiles.** Assign each company to a profile (bank, insurance, REIT, utility, cyclical or default). The profile decides which metrics apply (§5).
4. **Red flags.** Detect deterioration and risk signals, each with a severity (§6).
5. **Screens.** Apply gates, then score companies with peer-relative percentiles across weighted pillars, then grade confidence (§4).
6. **Outputs.** Write ranked lists, a needs-review list, exclusions, flags, summary statistics and charts.

## 2. Investable universe (`settings.yaml → universe`)

| Rule | Default |
|---|---|
| Security type | Common stock only. ETFs, funds, preferreds, warrants, units, rights and ADRs are excluded. ADRs are optional via `include_adrs` |
| Share class | Secondary classes are excluded (keep GOOGL, drop GOOG) |
| Exchanges | NYSE, NASDAQ, NYSE American, NYSE Arca, Cboe BZX |
| SPACs / blank checks | SIC 6770 and name patterns excluded |
| Market cap | ≥ $500M |
| Price | ≥ $5 |
| Liquidity | Median daily dollar volume (60 days) ≥ $2M |
| History | ≥ 3 fiscal years |
| Freshness | Latest filing ≤ 460 days old |
| Revenue | Latest FY revenue > 0 (excludes pre-revenue companies) |

Every excluded company appears in `excluded.csv` with all the reasons it failed.

## 3. Metrics and status codes

The full list is in [DATA_DICTIONARY.md](DATA_DICTIONARY.md). Conventions:

- **Time alignment.** Offset 0 is the latest fiscal year and offset 3 is three fiscal years earlier. Offsets come from period-end dates, so a missing year leaves a gap instead of shifting the series.
- **Stock splits.** Share counts, diluted EPS and dividends per share are put on today's share basis before any multi-year comparison. Data sources often restate only recent years, so a 4-for-1 split would otherwise look like a 75% dividend cut.
  - **How a split is detected:** a year-over-year share-count jump that matches a standard split ratio (3:2 up to 50:1, or a reverse split), unless assets or revenue jumped by a similar amount (a stock-funded acquisition), or the company's other share-count series shows no matching split. Mergers phase into weighted-average shares over two years, so they rarely produce a clean ratio in both series.
  - **How per-share values are fixed:** each EPS or DPS value is checked against net income or dividends paid ÷ adjusted shares, and divided by the split factor only if it is still on the old basis.
  - The `splits_detected` column shows how many splits were adjusted.
- **Current values.** These use trailing-twelve-month (TTM) data when it is newer than the latest FY, otherwise the latest FY. Trends (CAGRs, margin changes) always compare fiscal years.
- **Growth.** CAGR is `(end / start)^(1/n) − 1`. It is **not meaningful (NM)** when either end is ≤ 0, so a recovery from losses is not reported as a fake 500% growth rate.
- **Robust EPS growth.** A plain CAGR uses two years, so a write-off, impairment, hedge mark-to-market loss or cyclical trough in the starting year (or a one-off gain or peak) dominates it. `eps_trend_growth_pct` is the annualized **Theil-Sen** slope of log diluted EPS over up to 8 fiscal years (FY0..FY-7): the median of all pairwise growth rates, which tolerates about 29% distorted years (two of eight, often three). It needs the latest year and ≥5 positive years; it is NM when the latest year is a loss or two or more years are losses, and "short" with fewer than 5 years. 
- **Scored growth = recent where trustworthy, robust otherwise.** The 8-yr trends lag a recent change in growth (Apple: trend 14.7%/yr vs 3-yr CAGR 6.9%). Scoring therefore uses blends: `eps_growth_scored_pct`, `ebitda_growth_scored_pct`, `fcf_growth_scored_pct`, `operating_leverage_scored_pp`, `operating_margin_change_scored_pp`, `gross_margin_change_scored_pp` are the average of the plain 3-yr figure and the robust trend when the 3-yr figure is trustworthy (status ok), and the robust trend alone when its base year is distorted or near zero. Apple's scored EPS growth is 10.8%; Nvidia's 3-yr CAGR starts from its FY2023 trough, so its scored growth is the trend (82%/yr). A CAGR whose base is below 5% of the latest value is NM (a business turning profitable). `rule_of_40_pct` (1-yr revenue growth + FCF margin) is shown as reference only, because both parts are already scored.
- **Starting-year distortion check (plain CAGRs, reference only).** For the 3y and 5y EPS CAGRs, the starting year is compared with the Theil-Sen trend fitted through every *other* year in the window (`eps_base_vs_trend_3y_x`, `_5y_x`). Outside 0.67–1.5x, the starting year is clearly abnormal and the CAGR is marked **NM** while its value stays visible (the company view shows `[nm: distorted base year — reference only]`). Example (SEC GAAP data): Duke's 3y EPS CAGR of 25.8% starts from FY2022 EPS of $3.17, 0.64x its trend; the robust trend is 7.4% (adjusted EPS grew ~4%/yr). The check is general — it applies to every profile — and is not applied to 1-yr growth, which is meant to show the latest year's change.
- **Operating income, EBITDA and margins get the same treatment.** Scored: robust trends of operating income and EBITDA (`opinc_trend_growth_pct`, `ebitda_trend_growth_pct`), robust operating leverage (op-income trend minus revenue trend), and Theil-Sen margin slopes ×3 (`operating_margin_trend_3y_pp`, `gross_margin_trend_3y_pp`, read like a 3-yr change). Reference only: the plain op-income/EBITDA CAGRs (NM when the FY-3 base is more than 1.5x off the other years' trend) and the plain margin changes (NM when the base-year margin is more than max(3pp, 3× the other years' typical deviation) from their trend; `op_margin_base_vs_trend_3y_pp` shows the gap). The margin-deterioration flag reads the robust trend when the 3-yr change starts from an abnormal year. Example: NextEra's +14.4pp 3-yr operating-margin change starts from a hedge-loss year and is NM. FCF CAGRs get the same checks (base > 1.5x off trend, or < 5% of latest → NM) and a robust log trend over the positive years (NM with 2+ negative years): Nvidia's 194%/yr 3-yr FCF CAGR starts from its FY2023 trough and is NM; its scored FCF growth is the 92%/yr trend.
- **Normalized valuation.** `pe_normalized_x`, `ev_ebitda_normalized_x` and `fcf_yield_normalized_pct` use normalized earnings: the **average margin over up to 8 fiscal years (losses included) × the latest revenue**. Averaging the margin strips out the boom/bust in profitability; using today's revenue avoids penalizing a company that has grown (a constant-margin grower has normalized P/E = trailing P/E). `earnings_vs_normalized_x` = latest net income / normalized net income. A trend line through EPS was tested and rejected: when a boom ends the window it reads as growth, so it called Nucor's 2023 near-peak earnings below normal.
- **Operating income.** When a filing doesn't tag operating income (common for REITs and holding companies), EBIT = pretax income + interest expense is used instead, excluding property-sale gains and impairments.
- **ROIC.** EBIT × (1 − tax rate) ÷ average invested capital, where invested capital is debt + equity − cash. The tax rate is the effective rate when it falls between 0 and 50%, otherwise 21%.
- **Leverage.** Net debt ÷ EBITDA. A negative value means net cash. The ratio is NM when EBITDA ≤ 0.
- **Valuation.** Multiples use market cap or EV against TTM/FY figures, and are NM when the denominator is ≤ 0. The P/E and EV/EBITDA comparisons with history divide the current multiple by the median FY-end multiple of the prior five years (at least three needed).
- **Dividends.** Coverage is measured as dividends ÷ FCF on a three-year cumulative basis, which smooths lumpy cash flows.
  - **Validation:** reported DPS is checked against dividends paid ÷ shares. A value more than 2× off (a mis-tagged fact) is discarded and the calculated figure from reported totals is used instead.
  - **Special dividends:** a year whose DPS is more than 1.4× both neighbouring years is treated as a special. It still counts as a paying year but is skipped in growth, streak and cut tests; a growth rate whose base year is a special is left missing.
  - **Cuts:** a dividend cut means regular DPS fell by more than 10% in any of the last five FYs, **and** dividends paid per share fell too (by more than 3%) whenever both years have it. A DPS figure out of line with the cash actually paid is not a cut (one Sharadar REIT: DPS −24% while dividends paid per share rose 3%).
- **PEG** = trailing P/E / EPS growth (scored), not analyst estimates. Growth below 3%/yr makes the ratio explode, so PEG is NM there; growth above 40%/yr is capped at 40 in the denominator, because trailing hyper-growth is not a sustainable rate. Forward P/E is an optional module that only works when an estimates table is supplied.

| Status | Meaning | How scoring treats it |
|---|---|---|
| `ok` | computed | percentile rank |
| `nm` | not meaningful (negative denominator or base) | 0.00 for lower-is-better metrics (e.g. P/E with losses), 0.25 for higher-is-better. Overridable per metric |
| `missing` | an input is absent | 0.35 (mildly below neutral) and **reduces data coverage** |
| `short` | not enough history for the look-back | same as missing |
| *(n/a)* | metric doesn't apply to the profile | **dropped** and the remaining weights are re-spread |

The screener never fills in a missing raw financial figure.

## 4. Scoring model (`scoring.py`)

1. **Gates.** A company must pass a screen's gates before it is scored.
   - Growth: 3-yr revenue CAGR ≥ 8% and 1-yr growth ≥ 0.
   - Income: yield ≥ 1.5% and at least 3 consecutive dividend years.
2. **Percentiles.** Each metric becomes a mid-rank percentile from 0 (worst) to 1 (best) among gate-eligible companies.
   - Weighting is **70% rank within peer group + 30% rank across the universe**.
   - The peer group is the special-industry profile, otherwise the sector. A group with fewer than 8 members falls back to the universe.
   - Percentiles are robust to outliers, so extreme values can't dominate.
3. **Pillars.** A pillar score is the weighted average of its metric scores. Correlated metrics share a pillar so they aren't double-counted.
4. **Composite.** The weighted average of pillar scores, scaled 0–100.
5. **Flag penalties.** Each *moderate* flag subtracts 4 points, capped at 20. A *severe* flag moves the company to `needs_review` instead of ranking it.
6. **Confidence.** Data coverage is the weighted share of applicable metrics that were computed.
   - Grades: A ≥ 90%, B ≥ 75%, C ≥ 60%.
   - Below 60%, the company goes to `needs_review` with reason "insufficient data coverage".

### Growth screen weights

| Pillar | Weight | Metrics (relative weight) |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 30 | revenue CAGR 3y (1.5), 5y (0.75); EPS growth, scored (1); FCF growth, scored (0.75, noisy with capex cycles); EBITDA growth, scored (0.25, largely overlaps EPS growth); revenue acceleration (0.5) |
| Operating leverage | 10 | operating leverage, scored (1); gross-margin change, scored (0.5). Operating-margin change is not scored here: it carries the same information as operating leverage |
| Profitability | 20 | ROIC (1.5); gross margin (0.75); operating margin (1); FCF margin (1); FCF conversion (0.75) |
| Financial strength | 15 | net debt/EBITDA (1.5, lower); interest coverage (1); current ratio (0.5); equity/assets (0.5) |
| Valuation | 15 | EV/sales (0.75), EV/EBITDA (1), FCF yield (1), PEG (1), P/E (0.5). All lower-is-better except FCF yield |
| Capital allocation | 10 | share-count CAGR 3y (1, lower); SBC/revenue (1, lower). Each measures something different: SBC is the cost of stock pay whether or not buybacks offset it; share count is the net dilution. FCF is not reduced by SBC anywhere scored (`fcf_margin_ex_sbc_pct` is reference only), so stock pay is penalized once. The `high_sbc` flag is informational when the `dilution` flag already carries a penalty |

| Pillar | Weight | Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Dividend safety | 30 | FCF payout 3y (1.5), earnings payout (1), net debt/EBITDA (1), interest coverage (0.75), dividend cut (0.75) |
| Quality & stability | 20 | ROIC (1); profitable years (0.75); FCF-positive years (0.75); margin volatility (0.75, lower); margin change 3y (0.5) |
| Dividend growth | 15 | DPS CAGR 5y (1), 3y (0.75); growth streak (0.75) |
| Yield | 15 | dividend yield (1); shareholder yield (1) |
| Valuation | 15 | P/E (1), P/FCF (0.75), EV/EBITDA (0.75), P/E vs own 5-yr median (0.5) |
| Growth | 5 | revenue CAGR 5y (1), EPS growth, scored (1) |

Yield is only 15% of the income score. Safety, quality and valuation together carry 65%.

## 5. Special industries (`industry_profiles.yaml`)

| Profile | Match | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Bank | SIC 6020–6036, 6120–6129, "bank" industries, mortgage REITs | Drops EV, EBITDA, FCF, current ratio, interest coverage, gross/operating margin and ROIC. **Cash, total debt, net debt and debt/equity are also n/a:** a bank is funded mostly by deposits and its SEC debt tags capture only part of its borrowings, so those figures would mislead. Uses ROA, **ROE on average common equity** (preferred stock excluded where reported, matching how banks report ROE), equity/assets, P/TBV and P/B. Leverage and FCF flags are disabled. Metrics that don't apply show as `n/a for bank` in the company view and are blank in outputs. **Not available in the standardized feed:** net interest margin, efficiency ratio, CET1, credit losses **Also applied to bank-funded financials:** a company with SIC 6000–6299 and equity below 15% of assets (broker-dealers like Goldman Sachs at ~8%, card issuers like American Express) moves from the default profile to bank treatment, because its debt funds financial assets; asset-light financials (Visa, exchanges, asset managers) stay default. |
| Insurance | Carriers: SIC 6310–6399 and "insurance" industries, **excluding brokers/agents** (SIC 6411 and broker industries, which are scored as normal fee businesses) **and managed-care plans** (SIC 6324 / "Healthcare Plans", e.g. UnitedHealth: service businesses with meaningful FCF) | Premiums are invested before claims are paid, so operating cash flow includes float and the investment portfolio, not "cash", backs the liabilities. **n/a:** FCF metrics, cash, net debt, debt/equity, EV and EBITDA metrics, gross margin, current ratio, ROIC and ending-equity ROE. **Uses:** ROE on average common equity; **book value per share** and its growth, reported and **ex-AOCI** (ex-AOCI strips interest-rate-driven unrealized bond gains/losses); P/B and P/TBV on common equity; **debt/capital** for leverage (flag above 35%, severe above 45%); EBIT interest coverage for holding-company debt; earnings payout. Treated as cyclical (underwriting cycle), so margins far above the 10-year average are flagged. Validated: Travelers BVPS $151.24 vs $151.21, debt/capital 22.0% vs 22.0%, ROE 20.6% vs 21.0% (Travelers averages equity quarterly); Chubb BVPS $188.59 vs $188.59, BVPS growth 18.0% vs 18.0%, ex-AOCI growth 11.0% vs 11.0%. **Not available:** combined ratio, catastrophe losses, reserve development, float, core ROE (which also excludes realized gains). "Adjusted" book value differs by company: Travelers excludes only unrealized investment gains/losses ($158.01 vs our ex-AOCI $162.73), Chubb all AOCI |
| REIT | SIC 6798, "REIT" (a SIC 6798 match also needs a REIT industry label: former REITs such as CoreCivic keep the code) | GAAP earnings are depressed by real-estate depreciation, so the earnings-based measures are replaced: **P/FFO** instead of P/E, **FFO payout** instead of earnings payout, **FFO/share growth** instead of EPS growth, **net debt/EBITDAre** instead of net debt/EBITDA, and **EBITDAre/interest** instead of EBIT/interest. Also n/a: P/E-based metrics, ROE, gross margin, current ratio, FCF/net income and profitable-years. **FFO (NAREIT)** = net income to common + D&A + real-estate impairments − gains on property sales (it falls back to net income + D&A, flagged by `ffo_nareit_adjusted = 0`, when impairment/gain data are missing). **EBITDAre** = EBITDA + impairments − gains, trailing (management usually quotes an annualized pro forma figure). Dividend coverage is therefore **FFO payout** (the weak-coverage flag fires when dividends exceed FFO), valuation is **P/FFO**, and cash-flow growth is **FFO/share growth**, for every REIT, so the peer group shares one definition. Gross margin (not reported) and current ratio (negative working capital is normal) are also n/a. **Uses:** earnings payout (GAAP); dividend growth; **CFO/debt**, a proxy for the rating agencies' FFO/debt, in the financial-strength and dividend-safety pillars; net debt/EBITDA, ranked against utility peers only; P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B and yield. **Flags:** the leverage flag uses CFO/debt (below 12%, severe below 8%) rather than net debt/EBITDA, because 5–6.5x is normal for utilities. Interest coverage is flagged below 2.0x (severe below 1.2x). NextEra tags only cash interest paid ($3.5B; reported interest expense was $4.6B including derivative losses). Rate base and allowed ROE are not available |
| Cyclical | energy, materials, chemicals, steel, semis, autos, homebuilders (SIC 1520–1531), construction/mining machinery (SIC 3530–3537), shipping | **Exit rule:** the industry is a prior, not a verdict. A company whose own record shows an 8-yr revenue trend ≥ 15%/yr and at most one revenue decline in 5 years is scored as a general operating company (Nvidia: 55.5%/yr, no declines; the company view prints the reason). Nucor, Chevron and ExxonMobil stay cyclical. | dilution | share count CAGR > 3%/yr (severe > 8%) | moderate / severe |

| negative_equity | equity < 0 | info (ROE and P/B become NM) |

| short_history | < 5 fiscal years | info |

## 7. Known limitations

- **Rankings are relative.** A score of 70 means "better than most peers on these metrics", not "undervalued".
- **Standardized data has limits.** Bank and insurance KPIs aren't available, and the REIT FFO figure is approximate.
- **Forward estimates are optional.** Until an estimates table is added, PEG uses trailing growth.
- **Scored growth still leans on history.** The blends halve, but do not remove, the lag of the 8-yr trend; a company whose growth just changed direction is scored partly on its past. Revenue CAGRs and 1-yr growth carry the most recent signal.
- **Revenue definitions differ by filer in SEC data.** ExxonMobil tags only `Revenues` = total revenues *and other income* ($332,238M, incl. $5,064M equity-affiliate income and $3,269M other income); its sales line ($323,905M) has no standard tag. Chevron tags sales separately ($184,432M). XOM's margins are therefore understated by ~2.5% relative and its revenue overstated by 2.6%. Sharadar's standardized revenue should be checked against this in the sample step.
- **REIT FFO is a standardized NAREIT approximation.** It uses total D&A (not only real-estate D&A), consolidated figures with no joint-venture share or noncontrolling-interest adjustment, and no discontinued-operations adjustment. Final reconciliation of 33 REITs against company-reported FY2025 NAREIT FFO/share: 26 within 10% and a median absolute gap of 4.7%. The 7 above 10% each reconcile line by line to one of those items: Equinix +31% (non-real-estate D&A), Iron Mountain +106% on FY2025 (real-estate depreciation is $421.6M of $1,024.4M D&A), American Tower +12% (NCI adjustments), Simon −16% and Prologis −12% (JV depreciation), Crown Castle −35% (discontinued-operations loss) and Terreno −10.5% (vendor D&A field). See [REIT_FFO_RECONCILIATION.md](REIT_FFO_RECONCILIATION.md).
- **Normalized earnings need history.** With fewer than 8 fiscal years the average may not span a full cycle; with fewer than 5 it is unavailable. Revenue itself is cyclical (steel and oil prices), so at a peak normalized earnings are still somewhat high.
- **Revenue growth is not cycle-adjusted.** Revenue CAGRs (and the Growth gate) still compare start and end years; for cyclicals the peak flag, severe in Growth, is the safeguard.
- **Trailing data looks backward.** It can't see guidance changes or events after the latest filing.
- **SEC mode is for development and validation.** Its security classification is heuristic and it has no prices unless you supply `--prices-file`.
- **Sharadar vs SEC (free DJIA-30 sample, 5 fiscal years, checked 2026-10-08).** Net income, operating cash flow, equity, total assets and dividends paid matched SEC 100% (median difference 0% on every field except debt). Definition differences, not errors: Sharadar's **debt** includes lease liabilities (MSFT +$16.5B, NVDA +$2.6B, HD +$11.5B over SEC - inferred from the gaps) and bank funding (JPM; bank debt is n/a anyway); oil-major **revenue** is total revenues *and other income* (CVX $189,031M vs $184,432M sales), which puts CVX and XOM on the same basis; **DPS** follows dividends paid in the fiscal year (JPM $5.55 vs $5.80 declared); **capex** definitions vary (CAT includes equipment leased to customers; CVX $15.5B vs $17.3B reported); per-share history is restated to the current share basis (Honeywell's reverse split: EPS $14.70 vs SEC $7.36, net income identical). Adapter rules: SBC reported as exactly 0 is treated as missing (JPM, CAT, TRV disclose $3.6B / $242M / $256M); filing dates come from the as-reported rows (ARY/ART), because Sharadar dates the restated MRY/MRT rows by report period.
- **Spin-offs and large special dividends.** After a spin-off the price reflects a smaller company while trailing earnings, EBITDA and dividends still include the spun-off business, so P/E, EV/EBITDA and yield look falsely cheap (Honeywell 2026: P/E 8.0, yield 4.7% vs a 1.9% 5-yr median; it ranked #1 in Income before this rule). `largest_distribution_1y_pct` measures the biggest one-day step between the split-adjusted and fully adjusted close; above 8% the company is flagged `recent_distribution` (severe → needs review). Needs both price series (Sharadar; not available from SEC data).
- **SEC parsing rules worth knowing (2).** Basic EPS / basic weighted shares fill only years with no diluted figure (Lennar FY2025 tags only basic; basic is ≥ diluted, usually by <1%). When the noncurrent long-term-debt line sits next to short-term borrowings and no `DebtCurrent` total is tagged, current maturities are added (Nucor $7,121M, AbbVie 2018 $40,310M = $36,611M long-term incl. current + $3,699M short-term); with a `DebtCurrent` total present, the short-term line already includes them (Chevron). Foreign-currency remeasurement gains/losses are removed from the EBIT fallback used when no operating-income line is tagged (Public Storage 2025 FX loss $216M); equity-affiliate income stays in. Net income is income available to common shareholders (after participating securities), consistent with diluted EPS - e.g. Nucor $1,737M vs $1,744M attributable.

- **Dividend history rules.** DPS is the reported tag, or dividends paid / diluted shares when the tag is missing or wrong (more than 2x off; or a one-year >10% outlier while the years either side agree within 3% - P&G FY2017 $3.00 tagged vs $2.64 paid). A calculated value in a year the share count moves >25% (stock-paid merger: Chubb 2016) is dropped, and one unknown year between two known years is bridged with their geometric midpoint when the dividend rose across it. In streaks, a change within ±1.5% between years whose DPS is calculated is treated as continuing (Chevron 2017: -0.07%); real cuts (>10%) are still detected. Non-payers show dividend growth as n/a (`[n/a: no dividend]`), not NM.
- **SEC parsing rules worth knowing.** A filer with no common-dividend facts of any kind (e.g. Adobe) records dividends as 0, not missing, so its yield is 0 and shareholder yield is computed; one dividend fact anywhere keeps a gap a gap. When a ticker moves to a new registrant CIK (a holding-company reorganization, e.g. ExxonMobil Holdings Corp in 2026), history is merged from the predecessor CIK, found from the accession numbers of the new registrant's own 10-Q/10-K filings and accepted only when the name or business address matches (override: `providers.sec.predecessor_ciks`). Commercial paper is added to debt only when no short-term-borrowings total is tagged (it is normally inside that line). `LongTermDebtAndCapitalLeaseObligations` is a noncurrent line, so when it is the only long-term tag, current debt is added (`DebtCurrent`, else current maturities + commercial paper): ExxonMobil $43,537M and Coca-Cola now match their balance sheets. When only cash *including* restricted cash is tagged, tagged restricted cash is subtracted.
